I captained FIRST Tech Challenge Team 14450 RoboRebels from 2022 to 2026. Over those seasons I designed, built and programmed three competition robots, with linear slide, arm and flywheel auto-targeting systems.

This team was my passion in high school. I spent more time working on the robots than on my classes.

We started out as nobodies: we had never made it past our local competitions, and I wanted to change that. With my brother's help I learned a lot about CAD and good programming practice, and we made it out of our league and into the state championship.

From there we just kept getting better. The next season we nearly won states, losing the final 2–1 in a very close series, and advanced to the Mexico Premier Event, where we went undefeated and won the whole event. That season we also competed at the Maryland Tech Invitational against some of the best teams in the world, and learned a ton.

In my final year as captain I set out to build the best robot in the state, and we held that spot right up to the state championship. We made the finals there but didn't win. We still advanced to the World Championship, where we put up a good fight with a fully rebuilt robot.

## Control systems

Over my time in robotics I worked with a lot of control and programming concepts, including PID, feedforward, inverse kinematics, trajectory mapping, localization, odometry and path planning.

My favorite came in my senior year, when the game had us shooting balls into a goal. We did the physics derivations ourselves and implemented full auto-targeting that automatically leads the aiming angle and adjusts the shooter's speed, so however the robot was moving, the balls would land in the goal.
